Interreg Upper Rhine

Borderless E-Mobility – A Federated Charging Park for Sustainable Freight Transport in the Upper Rhine

eTruckCharge is investigating how charging capacity that already exists at commercial sites can be made usable across company and national borders — enabling a safe, plannable shift to electric trucks for the trinational logistics sector.

  • 07/2026 – 06/2029Project duration
  • €1,311,414.60EU co-financing (ERDF)
  • €2,866,829.21Total budget
  • 16 partnersfrom Germany, France, Switzerland

Background

About the project

Sustainable road freight transport must combine shorter charging times, cost efficiency and environmental sustainability. In Germany alone, road freight transport consumes around 15 million tonnes of fossil fuel every year. Battery-electric trucks already achieve ranges of more than 500 km today, with charging times from 20% to 80% state of charge in under an hour.

Cross-border freight flows between France, Germany and Switzerland in the Upper Rhine region rely mainly on road transport — in particular via the A4, A6/A36 and A5 motorways. However, no reliable, high-power charging infrastructure for electric trucks is currently available around the clock at these hubs.

eTruckCharge addresses exactly this gap: the project investigates and develops how charging capacity that already exists or is planned at commercial sites — logistics centres, warehouses, vehicle depots, industrial parks — can be made usable across company and national borders through a "federated charging park."

Our ambition

Project objectives

Companies with their own charging stations will be able to open them up in a controlled way to business partners and other users via an IT system developed in the project, and make them bookable. This allows hauliers to pre-reserve charging capacity along their routes and plan cross-border tours safely and efficiently.

The project also develops intelligent energy management for the charging sites: AI-based forecasting and control of charging demand (up to 1 MW per site), taking into account electricity market prices and grid contracts as well as vehicle-specific requirements such as state of charge and destination.

Security of the overall system — at both data and infrastructure level — is a central concern. To this end, eTruckCharge is developing a collaborative system for detecting attacks and anomalies based on federated learning, without directly sharing sensitive data.

Results

What the project delivers

Direct and indirect results available during and after the project period.

Software system for the federated charging park

A web-based system developed by Hochschule Karlsruhe: charging stations at multiple company sites are pooled and can be reserved by authorised third parties for a defined period.

AI models & digital twin

Models trained via federated learning predict station occupancy, arrival/departure times and load peaks without centralising raw data. A digital twin represents the charging park in real time.

Charging algorithms by RPTU

Optimised charge planning and fast-charging profiles, validated in a Power-Hardware-in-the-Loop environment; released as an open-source package with standardised interfaces.

Security & resilience

A collaborative intrusion-detection system based on federated learning to protect the connected charging infrastructure, vehicles and systems.

Impact

Target groups & benefits

In the medium term, the results will benefit potential users in the Upper Rhine logistics sector ("first movers") as well as future providers of scalable solutions from the region, including start-ups.

The project can also help position the Upper Rhine region as a pioneer in the systematic introduction of electric vehicles in cross-border freight transport — improving air quality, reducing CO₂ emissions, and strengthening the region's resilience against dependency on oil-exporting countries.
